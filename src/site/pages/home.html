The Horizontal Front

A satirical rhythm game about working extremely hard to do absolutely nothing.

Management wants you vertical. You want to stay exactly where you are. Hold the line to a rhythm of rubber stamps, typewriters and pneumatic tubes while a grotesque embodiment of capitalism hauls on a brass lever bolted to the head of your bed. Under this arrangement, even lying down is labour.

Hold the line How the commons works

Let me remain in bed

This is political satire about compulsory productivity: the working day’s habit of treating your rest as an unclaimed resource, your attention as inventory, and your Monday morning as something it is owed.

So the game makes the joke literal. Staying horizontal is gruelling, skilled, rhythmic work. You will sweat to achieve nothing whatsoever. That contradiction is the argument: a system that can turn lying down into a full-time job can turn anything into one.

Losing is called forced verticalisation. It is funny, and then it is not.

Management will be in touch

Management is not a person. Management is a role — a fictional grotesque in a pinstripe suit, straining at the lever, never a caricature of any particular politician, executive or other real human being. The target is the arrangement, not the individual.

Their methods will be familiar:

  • Just a Quick CallComplete three excuses in the correct order. Wrong excuse and the call connects.
  • Urgent EmailMark it unread before it opens. Let go too early and it opens.
  • The working day itselfPressure that rises however well you play, because the clock is Management’s weapon rather than your performance.

The same argument, told straight

A game about resisting extraction would be a poor joke if the game itself were extractive. A free, open-source game about collective power, mutual aid and resistance to hierarchical systems ought to be governed like one, so The Horizontal Front is built as a digital commons: freely accessible, community-supported, and held to a public charter that is deliberately difficult to overturn.

No advertising, because your attention is not ours to sell back to you. No analytics, because your Monday morning is not data. Copyleft code and share-alike cultural work, so that nobody — including us — can fence it off later.

Development, proposals and decisions are public in the GitHub repository.
