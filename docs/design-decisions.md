# Design decisions

This record collects settled product decisions and the reasoning behind them.
It is authoritative for design direction; the original discussion is preserved
unchanged in [history](history/expanded-design-direction.md).

## The duvet communicates danger

The duvet, bed and bedroom are the primary danger interface rather than a
conventional meter. As management gains ground, the duvet moves down the
player's body, the mattress tilts, cold work light enters the room, office
clutter appears and the exposed sleeper struggles to remain horizontal.
Successful resistance restores cover and warmth.

This makes safety physical and emotional: the player is losing comfort,
privacy, warmth and control. A small accessibility indicator may supplement the
scene, but must not replace the duvet as the principal communication channel.

## Rhythm is authored, not arbitrary mashing

Resistance follows authored patterns judged by side, action and timing. Touch,
keyboard and pointer input map into the same domain actions. Unstructured rapid
input is not the mechanic; rhythm accuracy builds persistent resistance which
suppresses the corresponding fraction of management pressure between beats.

The finite rhythm vocabulary supports taps, explicit rests, sustained holds,
waltz groupings, syncopation and call-and-response. A shared musical clock may
drive synthetic music, but control-local visual targets and optional haptics
must express the same timing so sound is never the only critical cue.

Complex rhythms must be introduced and rehearsed before they are tested. The
first episode therefore teaches straight alternation, then sustained grips,
then a three-actions-and-rest phrase before a continuous alternating crisis.
More complex catalogue rhythms belong in episodes designed to teach them.

Difficulty may increase through pattern complexity, timing tolerance, recovery,
pressure, interruptions and combinations. It must not primarily become a race
toward higher input rates. A slow authored beat remains mechanically viable
because danger is governed by net pressure against earned resistance rather
than by unavoidable damage between discrete healing opportunities. This is both
a game-rule decision and a protection against repetitive strain.

Presentation intensity is independent from physical danger. Atmosphere may
become more alarming while the bed, duvet and sleeper continue to report actual
safety. Accurate crisis play can therefore visibly arrest or reverse the lift.

## Winning grants time, not an economy

The player wins more time in bed. The game does not convert resistance into
coins, upgrades, optimisation, daily rewards or a progression economy. Such a
system would reproduce the productivity ideology being satirised.

Anti-rewards may celebrate reclaimed time and the absence of obligation:

- Five More Minutes.
- A Warm Patch of Mattress.
- No New Responsibilities.
- Temporary Absence of Notifications.
- The Satisfaction of Non-Compliance.
- A Pillow Flipped to the Cool Side.

They may be presented with the pomp of achievement rewards, but do not become
currency or mechanical advantage. Cosmetic bedding is also excluded from the
initial direction because collection can quietly reintroduce grinding and
acquisition. Any future appearance choices must remain lightweight, freely
available and unrelated to repetitive progression.

## The Orange Fella is a recurring boss

One recurring fictional boss is stronger than a sequence of disposable
managers. He embodies capital's ability to survive every embarrassment and
rebrand the same demand as transformation. He may return under titles such as
Founder and CEO, Chief Motivation Officer, Majority Shareholder, Productivity
Mentor, Chairman of the Alarm or Secretary for Forced Verticalisation.

Every defeat damages his dignity but not his confidence. A new tie, title,
initiative and explanation can vary the satire without pretending the system
has changed. His eventual name and final visual identity remain open creative
decisions; he must stay an original archetype rather than a reproduction of a
real person's face.

## Promotion is a trap, not an upgrade path

A promotion may offer immediate relief and then impose harder conditions, more
responsibility or further absurd demands. Refusing it is a meaningful act of
resistance. The mechanic satirises the bargain rather than opening a branching
career or upgrade system.

The important joke is that the impressive title supplies no useful power. Pay
may disappear into invented fees, workload may increase, and management may
describe the result as joining a family. Refusal deserves its own triumphant
recognition: **Career Opportunity Successfully Avoided.**

## Cartoon narrative carries the satire

Short illustrated sequences frame the arcade confrontation:

1. A campaign briefing or pre-confrontation cartoon announces the policy.
2. The rhythmic confrontation stages the struggle.
3. The accepted result delivers victory or forced-verticalisation prose and a
   visual punchline.
4. Occasional future sequences may introduce a larger absurdity.

The intended form is limited animated political cartooning: strong poses,
expressions, camera moves, speech bubbles and selective cut-out motion rather
than expensive frame-by-frame animation. This gives each confrontation a reason
and allows the story to span restructures, mergers, crises and other initiatives
while the bedroom remains recognisable.

## The protagonist is ordinary and determined

The protagonist begins gender-ambiguous, mostly concealed under the duvet, with
a simple expressive face, messy hair, ungendered pyjamas and warm rounded shapes
contrasting with management's sharp silhouette. They are visually ordinary
rather than aspirational or conventionally heroic.

Their defining quality is exhausted determination. They are not lazy; they
understand that their time belongs to them. Progressive exposure serves both
character economy and the danger mechanic. Lightweight appearance choices may
eventually exist, but not as purchases, rewards or collection pressure.

## Failure is funny, dramatic and sad

Forced verticalisation lands in three beats:

1. **Dramatic:** the duvet tears free and the bed reaches its vertical limit.
2. **Funny:** the sleeper arrives at an absurd workstation while management
   declares the return voluntary.
3. **Sad:** warmth drains away, fluorescent lights buzz and lost time becomes
   visible.

Pure slapstick would make the political point weightless. Failure should briefly
show the human cost—lost time, autonomy and energy—before inviting another act
of resistance. The game has no permanent victory and no nihilistic defeat: it
offers repeated moments of time reclaimed from the Orange Fella.

## Adaptive difficulty is not the default

Each confrontation owns an authored pressure and rhythm curve. Difficulty is a
creative property of that composition and should be understandable, testable
and comparable across input methods. Hidden adaptive difficulty would blur the
relationship between authored rhythm, player learning and mechanical outcome.

Accessibility options may deliberately alter timing, required input or motion,
and equivalent effort may require different thresholds for different devices.
Those explicit accommodations are not the same as an opaque system silently
changing the challenge in response to performance.
