Bureaucracy as percussion.

The Sound Library

Every sound in the game, free to listen to and free to reuse.

There are no audio files here. Each sound is built from a few lines of numbers the moment you press the button — a rubber stamp is two layers of maths, a phone is three. Nothing was sampled, nothing was recorded, and nothing needs clearing. The whole library is a few kilobytes of JSON.

The office is the orchestra

The score is played on the apparatus of the workplace it satirises. Rubber stamps keep the beat you are asked to answer; a typewriter answers it; a jammed key is a miss. Desk phones interrupt, pneumatic tubes strain, and a punch clock rings you out at the end of the day. The machinery that enforces the working day supplies the rhythm the player is resisting, which makes the soundtrack part of the argument rather than decoration over it.

Each episode draws its instrumentation from its own setting, so a different workplace sounds different without anyone writing new code.

The room, tightening

Underneath everything sits a bed that follows the drama. Drag the slider from rest to crisis: the hum climbs, the air-conditioning thickens, and the room closes in. In play this is driven by the episode's own dramatic curve.

Take it and use it

The library is licensed CC BY-SA 4.0, like the rest of the game's content. Because a sound is its parameters rather than a recording, reusing one means copying a small block of JSON — and changing one means editing a number and reloading. There is no digital audio workstation, no render step and no licensing to check.

If you want to build a campaign, the contribution guide is the place to start, and the audio grammar is documented in the repository's episode grammar reference.
